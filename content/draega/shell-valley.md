# Shell Valley: The Heartwood Descent

Out in the far northern reaches, the landscape stops making ordinary geological sense. You walk into a five mile wide basin with a mile wide lake sitting dead center, and rising straight out of a mound in the water is a thousand foot sequoia tree. Three small islands ring the base of the trunk like pebbles dropped into still water.

The first thing that hits your ears is the weight of the air. No birds call across the water, and no breeze stirs the canopy above. The whole valley is frozen in a heavy, primordial quiet that feels less like empty space and more like an audience holding its breath. The tree commands every inch of the ground.

## The Patron

Your contact for the descent is Grimbah, an ancient deep gnome druid. When you first meet him on the trail he looks like an ordinary traveler caked in dried river mud, right up until he shifts his shape into an enormous white bear.

He pays his gold upfront and asks no questions about where you came from or what you did yesterday. His requirement is simple on paper: he needs escorts and witnesses to accompany him to the center of the great sequoia for a primordial rite. The original text never clarifies what the rite prevents or what entity it addresses, which is the sort of omission that ought to make your teeth itch. Grimbah simply hands over the coin and assumes you know how to follow orders.

## The Four Rules

Before anyone touches the bark, Grimbah lays out the rules of the house. If someone asks him to repeat them, his patience evaporates immediately:

1. Take nothing that is not given to you.
2. Mind the snails.
3. This place is sacred.
4. Stick together.

His summary of the consequences is four words long: break them, and you get eaten. He means it.

## The Spiral

The interior of the sequoia contains no straight corridors and no tidy square corners. The entire route is a single path that spirals downward like the inner whorl of a giant snail shell.

The vertical descent runs one hundred feet deep beneath the roots, stepping through distinct elemental thresholds:

* The tree trunk exit at zero feet.
* The earth room at twenty five feet below the surface.
* The fire pit at fifty five feet down.
* The heart chamber at seventy five feet down.
* The water room at one hundred feet down.

## What the Wood Reveals

If you pay attention to the walls on the way down, the structure gives away its secrets. Foreign stones are wedged into the growth rings of the wood, worn smooth on top and placed by deliberate hands rather than grown naturally.

The entire spiral shaft acts as an enormous lung, pulling drafts of fresh air through the sequoia root network so the air never goes stale. Green bioluminescent moss clings to the bark and provides the only illumination as the shadows thicken. Earlier on the climb, two hundred and fifty feet up the trunk, Grimbah stops the party on a massive branch shelf for a mandatory rest. He does not sleep. He sits in complete silence, watching how everyone speaks and moves, measuring who will panic when the floor starts to drop.
