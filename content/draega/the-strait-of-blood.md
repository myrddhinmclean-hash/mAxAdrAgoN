# The Strait of Blood

The record gives it four sentences and then stops, which is the first interesting thing about it.

A large sandbar on the southeastern side of the island, surrounded by red rocks renowned for bleeding. Rumours of the medicinal qualities of that bleeding rock have circulated for centuries. It is said the rock can cure vampirism or lycanthropy. They are not sure which.

That last clause does more work than anything else on the page. Not "the rock cures both." Not "accounts differ." *They are not sure which.* Centuries of rumour and nobody has pinned down which curse the stone is supposed to lift, which tells you the rumour has never once survived contact with a result. If anybody had been cured, the story would have a name attached and a direction. It has neither.

Thousands of adventurers have tried to reach the strait hoping to recover the stone. Lost, and not heard from again. Every one of them.

The obvious reading is that the place is lethal, and it is, but that is not the part that holds up. A hazard that kills everyone who approaches also destroys the evidence that anything is there. The bleeding rock is famous entirely on the strength of testimony from people who never got close enough to test it, and the people who did get close are precisely the ones who cannot report back. The legend and the death toll are the same fact wearing two coats.

The hazards themselves are unromantic and stacked. Protruding rocks tear the hulls of ships and boats, and the shipwrecks that result are still there in the sandbar, which means every arrival passes the wreckage of the last one. Low tide traps sea life against the bar. Living on that trapped food is a local brand of carnivorous creature that has also learned to eat the people and adventurers who come looking for stone.

The record adds that they are particularly vicious and seem to be somewhat sentient.

*Somewhat sentient* is where a writer stops describing an animal and starts describing a problem. Vicious is a threat you plan around. Somewhat sentient is a threat that plans around you, and the hedge makes it worse rather than better, because it means whoever wrote that line was uncertain and put the uncertainty down anyway instead of picking the more comfortable answer.

Cursed individuals seek the rock. So do the giants, who want it for their own affliction, which is the detail that quietly tells you the giants consider themselves cursed. Nobody wrote that down as a fact about giants. It arrives sideways, in a sentence about who is shopping.

There is a series of caverns in the area. That is the last thing the record says, and it says it the way you would mention a cupboard.

Four sentences and a footnote about caves, and inside them a place that has killed everyone who ever went, for a cure nobody can name, guarded by animals that might be thinking. The gaps are not missing information. The gaps are the shape of the thing.
