# Nineteen Numbers With Nothing Behind Them

FlowGrid puts iron air at $79 per megawatt hour against LFP at $194 and vanadium at $173 across a hundred hour discharge, and every figure feeding those numbers arrived without a source.

The question is a good one. Lithium stops making economic sense somewhere past about eight hours, because its power cost and its energy cost are welded together in the same cell. Flow chemistries pull the two apart. The stack sets how fast, the tank sets how long, and a bigger tank is cheap. Where the crossover falls for a given site is worth knowing before anybody pours concrete.

The ordering is the product. Not the dollar figures, the order they arrive in.

Nineteen inputs sit under that ordering: capital costs, efficiencies, cycle lives. All of them were numbers in a file with nothing behind them. On 2026-09-03 every one was marked UNVERIFIED, with the NREL Annual Technology Baseline and Lazard named as the two sources that would settle them. An LFP cycle life of 4000 looks low against what is published, and it is flagged as looking low rather than quietly moved to something more flattering.

3 tests pass.

The output is worth exactly what the inputs are worth. Until somebody opens the ATB and checks all nineteen, that ordering is a hypothesis with a decimal point on it.
