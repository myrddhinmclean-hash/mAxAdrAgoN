# Nobody Wrote Down Which Way the Dose Was Wrong

AquaNutrient calculates a dose for a hydroponic reservoir, the number is wrong, and the note explaining how it is wrong is also wrong.

The problem it aims at is real enough. Field farming loses most of its fresh water to runoff and to the sky. Hydroponics wins that water back and charges for it in vigilance, because pH drifts quietly and a crop is ruined between two visits by nothing that looked like an event. So watch the reservoir and hand back the correction as one instruction carrying a number and a unit.

The number is uncalibrated, and the record of it contradicts itself. It says the dose reads about ten times low. In the same breath it says the program tells you to quarter the dose and titrate. Those point opposite ways. A figure ten times too small gets multiplied, and nobody wrote down which half of that sentence is the typo. Settling it now means pulling the project back out of the trash and running it, and until somebody does, the accurate statement is that the dose is wrong by an unknown factor in an unknown direction.

2 tests pass. It was the smallest thing in the register and there is no reason to pretend otherwise.

There is no hardware anywhere in it. No sensors, no pumps, nothing that touches water at any point, whatever the words closed loop and spectrometric suggest to somebody reading quickly.

A tool that prints its own error beside its own answer is not finished, it is honest, and those are different properties. This one printed an error bar pointing the wrong way, which is the third property and the worst of them.
