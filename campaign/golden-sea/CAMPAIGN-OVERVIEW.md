The Golden Sea Campaign — Expanded Campaign Overview
The Core Change: Time Becomes Real
The original sheet compresses a war into 38 days. That is the single biggest thing to fix, because the story you recorded is not a 38 day of a continent rising up, and continents do not rise in five weeks. So the first and biggest act of expansion is a time dilation: the campaign now spans roughly four years of world time, and the party lives inside those years. They winter somewhere. They train. They fail for a season and come back. Allies die of old age. Empire occupation hardens or cracks depending on how long the party takes.

The rule for the dilation is simple: every sea crossing is weeks, every city campaign is a season, every act is about a year. This does the narrative work for free. It explains why the Empire doesn't just crush Braaken (distance, weather, war on other fronts), why the party grows in power (years of adventuring, not weeks), and why the ending lands (they have been at this a long time and it cost them).

Campaign shape: 36 sessions, 4 hours each, three acts. Roughly one session per two weeks of table time early, slowing to month long arcs for the city campaigns.

Act One: The FairWhether Revolt (Sessions 1 to 8, Year One)
The party starts as nobody. Strangers at a market festival in FairWhether, a mountain city of dwarf furriers and oil merchants choking under Empire occupation. Empire Day goes wrong when Durgan's daughter is taken hostage, a lamp gets thrown, and the fire brigade's arrival turns a street fight into a legend. By nightfall the party is celebrated by the dwarves and hunted by the Empire, and there is no road back.

The act runs: guard duty and the spy hunt, the dock assault, the ent sting operation against the Empire navy, the boat defense, the dawn invasion that takes the lower city. Then the pivot: the rebellion cannot hold FairWhether against the Empire navy, so the party becomes couriers, carrying ITEM X and the Multipass out through the blockade, across the Golden Sea to Garegstown, where the Empire has begun a scorched earth withdrawal. The Golden Sea naval battle is the act's centerpiece: Empire reinforcements against a fishing fleet and a mysterious ally who walks on water using W1.

The act ends bitter, not triumphant. The road to Paradiso, the bandits, the execution of the wagoneer who helped them, jail, and a night escape arranged by Glori, who is funnier and more useful than any stranger should be.

New integration piece: the Empire is not cartoon evil, it is administrative evil. Occupation means grain requisitions, conscription lotteries, curfews, kobold auxiliaries who are mostly conscripts themselves. The party's first kill is a boy in a uniform. This is what makes the later PVP sessions land, because the question the campaign keeps asking is what you become while fighting this.

Act Two: The Long Crossing and Braaken (Sessions 9 to 20, Years Two and Three)
The middle of the campaign is a slow accumulation of allies, and it should feel like one. The party spends a full year in and around Braaken, and the module structure reflects it: some sessions are wars, some are jobs, some are a tournament and a festival.

The sea crossing brings the storm, the damaged Empire ship, and the waveborn parley: Glori reveals W1's purpose, the alliance is struck, and the party learns the waveborn have their own grievance, the Empire dredging the warm shallows where they raise their young. The escort to port AZ, Cpt. T's negotiation, and the disguised run to Braaken close year two.

Vision 1 hits the night before landfall.

Braaken is the heart of the campaign and now gets the room it deserves: ten sessions across a year of world time. Landing, the rebel leader, Glori's reveal as the prince. The docks, ITEM X and the dragon statue, the sinking of the Iron Fortress. The tournament of kings as a full season of the city's life, with EoE competing in it. The tunnels under the won land. The prison break. The solar event and the golem. The HQ siege, Glori freed, the Handler killed.

New integration pieces:
The rebel leader's death is no longer off screen. It happens between sessions, and the party spends two sessions finding out who ordered it: not the Empire, but a Braaken faction that feared the rebellion would hand the city to the waveborn. This is the campaign's first real moral brick, and it makes the PVP sessions make sense, because the same faction's agents are the rival group in the dreams.
The waveborn alliance has a price, stated at the parley and called due in Act Three: when Sortengraahd falls, the waveborn want the Empire's sea charts and the dredging stopped, and they want a seat at whatever table comes next. Whether the party honors this is a real choice.
EoE is the Empire's deniable instrument: a company of specialists, ex rebellion, ex anything, who do the jobs armies can't admit to. The Vision sender is not EoE. It is the vampire king, and the visions are the campaign's slow reveal of the true antagonist behind the Empire's fall: whoever taught the Empire that a dragon golem could be built, and wants the pieces when it falls.
Vision 1 (meeting) is moved to just before the tournament. It lands better when the party has a prize to lose.

Act Three: The Liberation March (Sessions 21 to 36, Year Four)
The original act three was six beach landings in eight weeks. Expanded, it is a year long campaign in four movements, and each movement gets its own regional identity:

Imnia (4 sessions). Not a landing, a wintering. The fleet overwinters at Imnia North Port, and the module set covers the frozen port, the snow road, and the discovery that the Empire has already fled toward the capital, burning what it can't carry. First contact with W4's existence and the first true vision of the vampire king, who finally has a face.

Sortengraahd (4 sessions, ending in PVP 2). The capital falls not to assault but to its own parliament voting to surrender, and the PVP is the fight over the succession: the rival party, now openly serving the vampire king, moves to take the throne before Glori can be crowned for real. Escape the castle with the proof, or die in it.

Bernax and Glazhenge (4 sessions). The Empire proper, the last loyal fleets and the mountain gate. The naval assault at Bernax is the waveborn's hour. Glazhenge's gates fall from inside, opened by prisoners the party freed three acts ago.

The Astral Plane (4 sessions, ending in PVP END). The vampire king's realm is not a dungeon, it is a negotiation, and the final PVP is the party split on what to do with him: kill him, bind him, or take his deal. The campaign's last question is the same as its first, asked of gods instead of kobolds: what are you willing to become to win?

The Spine, In One Paragraph Each
The W items: W1 talks to the drowned, W3 hides a ship, W2 marks a king, and W4 is the key to the Astral gate. All four are fragments of one thing the vampire king made and scattered, which is why he sends visions instead of armies: he cannot act until they are gathered, and the party is doing his gathering. Session 15's "learn of W4" is the campaign's turn.

ITEM X: the golem's heart seed. The Empire built the dragon golem to hold the sea, and ITEM X is what lets it wake, sleep, or obey. Who holds ITEM X at the end holds the scariest thing in the world. The sheet put it on a statue in Braaken Bay. It stays there. Act Three's final act is deciding what to do about that.

Glori: the prince who chose to be a helper instead of a ruler, and the campaign's slow question of whether the party crowned a king or made a friend. The PVP sessions are where that question gets answered, because in the endgame the party must decide whether Glori rules or the table does.

The Visions: three dreams, then a face, then a domain. Escalation by invitation. The vampire king never attacks first. He courts.

What This Gives You, Structurally
Four years of world time, 36 four hour sessions, a complete arc with an end you already recorded the first half of. The recorded sessions 0 to 15 map onto expanded Act One and early Act Two with beats intact, just roomier: the sheet's 38 days becomes about two years, and every location gets two to four sessions instead of one. Sessions 16 to 23 become Act Three's 16 sessions, which means you are no longer writing beach landings, you are writing regional campaigns.

The first module to build remains Session 1, FairWhether Market (the sheet's 0.5), now with room to breathe: market festival, the hostage taking, the fire, the brigade, the recruiting, and a closing scene the original never had, the party looking at the city gates they can no longer walk out of.
