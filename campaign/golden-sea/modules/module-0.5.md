# FairWhether Market

**Level:** 1
**Setting:** FairWhether market square and adjacent streets, a mountain city of dwarf furriers and oil merchants
**Main Threat:** Empire occupation, personified by the kobold guard captain Skritch, who holds the writ that day
**Adventure Summary:** The party walks into a mountain market on Empire Day and, within an hour, becomes a story the dwarves tell and a problem the Empire must solve. If the party does not move by nightfall, the Empire puts names on a list and the names include theirs.

---

## 1. Strong Start

> The plaza smells of lamp oil, hot bread, and wet cobble. A string of paper lanterns runs from the bakery awning to the tinker stall, and the wind pushes the smell of coal smoke sideways across three hundred dwarves who are pretending the Empire is not watching. At the center of the plaza, under a banner that says EMPIRE DAY in three languages, a man in a leather coat is reading a piece of paper to a lamp seller, and the lamp seller's daughter is being held by the arm by a kobold in uniform.

* **Active Obstacle (Gated Event):** A kobold guard detail of **eight kobolds** plus the human officer who holds the writ. The kobolds are not attacking. The kobolds are waiting. The writ is the lever, the daughter is the fulcrum, and the party has just walked into the geometry.
* **Tactical Objective:** Decide what the party does about the writ and the daughter in the next 3 rounds of in game time (about 6 real world minutes). If the party does nothing, the kobolds walk the daughter to the side gate and the writ goes with her.
* **Transition Trigger:** The lamp falls. The lamp breaks. The fire starts. The plaza is now a problem that moves.

---

## 2. Universal Hooks

The four hooks are rewritten from the engine template to fit the FairWhether campaign. None are rolled. All four are active in this session as facts the party can discover.

1. **The Bounty (rewritten as The Cousin's Debt).** A dwarf in the crowd, not identified, has been seen passing coin to a kobold. The liaison will tell the party, if asked at the meeting house, that Durgan's cousin owes a debt to an Empire-aligned merchant and the writ is the lever. Bounty equivalent: 25 gp to anyone who retrieves a small ledger from the Empire trading post before dawn.
2. **The Distress Call (rewritten as The Daughter).** The daughter of Durgan has been taken under a writ that names a back tax. The Empire claims the tax is real. The dwarves claim the tax is invented. The party is the only neutral party at the stall.
3. **The Unsealed Ruin (rewritten as The Side Gate).** A side gate in the plaza's east wall, usually locked, is propped open today for Empire Day foot traffic. The Empire officer plans to walk the daughter out through this gate. The party can reach the gate faster than the kobolds if they move now.
4. **The Direct Ambush (rewritten as The Banner Stand).** The EMPIRE DAY banner is hung from a wooden frame bolted to the cobbles. The frame is old, the bolts are rusted, and the banner rope has been cut on the Empire side. The frame is a trap waiting for a push.

## 3. Ten Secrets and Clues

Ten concrete facts about FairWhether, the revolt, Durgan, the liaison, and what the Empire is doing in the city. Each is discoverable in play through the listed check or social action. None require a dungeon.

1. **City Origin (rewritten from Dungeon Origin).** FairWhether was founded as a furrier's charter town in the third age of the dwarven calendar, on a granite shelf above a coal seam. The coal is why the city is wealthy. The granite is why the Empire built a garrison here. Discovery: **DC 10 Wisdom (History)** or asking the liaison at the meeting house.
2. **Structural Hazard (rewritten from Structural Hazard).** The east side gate's iron hinge pin is loose. A hard shove will drop the gate across the threshold, blocking the kobold exit for 1 round. Discovery: **DC 10 Wisdom (Perception)** when within 5 feet of the gate.
3. **Boss Vulnerability (rewritten from Boss Vulnerability).** Kobold Captain Skritch is afraid of fire. Skritch will not enter a room with a lit lamp in a tight space. Sunlight Sensitivity is inherited from the kobold base. Discovery: **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight)** the first time Skritch hesitates, or by reading the captain's body language at the gate.
4. **Faction Disruption (rewritten from Faction Disruption).** One of the eight kobolds in the writ detail, a young male with a red armband, is not actually Empire. He is a conscript who has been in the garrison for three months. If the party addresses him by name and offers a way out, he will drop his spear. Discovery: **DC 13 Charisma (Insight)** at the stall, or by noticing the armband is pinned on wrong.
5. **Secret Passage (rewritten from Secret Passage).** Durgan's stall has a back door that opens into a tanner's alley. The tanner's alley runs to the rear of the bakery. The bakery has a coal chute that descends to the cellars. The cellars connect, by way of an old steam vent, to the meeting house basement. Discovery: **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation)** at the back of Durgan's stall, or by following the daughter if the party saves her early.
6. **Impending Doom (rewritten from Impending Doom).** The Empire officer is expected back at the garrison with the daughter by sundown, no later than 6 in game hours from the start of the session. If he does not return, the garrison sends a squad. The squad arrives in 1 hour after the deadline. Discovery: **DC 10 Wisdom (Insight)** when the officer checks a pocket watch, or by asking the kobold with the red armband.
7. **Puzzle Solution (rewritten from Puzzle Solution).** The writ is sealed with Empire wax. The seal is pressed into a pattern of three concentric rings. The pattern matches the seal on the city charter, which the dwarves keep in a glass case at the inn. The pattern is a forgery. The pattern does not match the city charter. Discovery: **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation)** on the writ, or **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight)** if the party has seen the city charter seal.
8. **Guardian Lore (rewritten from Guardian Lore).** The dwarven fire brigade is not a guild. It is a hereditary obligation of the furrier clans, paid for by a city tax the Empire does not collect. The brigade answers to the city council, not the garrison. Discovery: asking the brigade captain at the fire, or **DC 10 Intelligence (History)**.
9. **Hidden Vault (rewritten from Hidden Vault).** The meeting house has a cellar. The cellar has a loose flagstone under the second barrel from the north wall. The flagstone covers a small iron box containing 50 gp, the liaison's emergency fund, and a list of names the party will see in the next session. Discovery: **DC 13 Wisdom (Perception)** in the cellar, or the liaison shows the party.
10. **The Next Threat (rewritten from The Next Threat).** The Empire officer's writ was not issued by the garrison commander. The writ was issued by a woman in a green coat at the trading post on the south side of the plaza, who is not Empire at all. She is a contractor for a faction the party will meet in Act Two. Discovery: **DC 13 Charisma (Investigation)** at the trading post after the fire, or the liaison reveals this at the meeting house.

## 4. Keyed Spatial Locations

The four keyed areas are mapped to the session's real locations. The first three contain non combat obstacles in place of monster encounters, per the integration rule that campaign canon wins where the engine shape would require combat in a festival crowd. Each non combat obstacle is statted with explicit stakes, checks, and failure consequences. The fourth area is the build to Boss Sanctum, retained as written.

### Area 1: The Market Square (Entry Point)

* **Dimensions and Environment:** 80 ft. by 60 ft. open cobble plaza, ringed by awnings on three sides, with a covered well at the north end and a hanging banner frame at the center. Lighting: bright afternoon sun. Smell: lamp oil, hot bread, coal smoke. Sound: a fiddler, a crowd murmur, the bark of a tinker.
* **Tactical Layout:** Two features. (1) The well at the north end provides 3/4 cover to anyone crouched behind it, and the cobbles around it are slick with splash water, giving disadvantage on Dexterity saving throws made within 5 feet of the well. (2) The banner frame at the center, a wooden tripod 12 ft. tall, is a structural hazard detailed in Area 3 if shoved or cut.
* **Obstacle (replaces Monster Encounter):** The Writ. The kobold detail is not a combat encounter; it is a bureaucratic and social one. The party can read the writ, talk to the kobold detail, address the Empire officer, or attempt to physically interpose between the kobolds and the daughter. Stakes: if the writ is accepted as legitimate, the daughter is gone. If the writ is refused, the kobold detail escalates to the officer, and the officer escalates to Skritch.
  * **Persuasion check to convince the kobolds to release the daughter without combat:** **DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion)** against the lead kobold, who is also a conscript. The kobold with the red armband will not enforce. Success: the daughter is released and the officer calls for Skritch. Failure: the kobolds tighten the hold and the writ is reinforced.
  * **Intimidation check to bluff authority over the Empire officer:** **DC 15 Charisma (Intimidation)** against the officer, who is not a villain. The officer is afraid of Skritch, not the party. Success: the officer defers to the party and asks for a written order. Failure: the officer calls for Skritch immediately.
* **Investigative DCs:**
  * **DC 10 Wisdom (Perception):** the kobold with the red armband is pinned on the wrong shoulder.
  * **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation):** the writ's wax seal is a forgery when compared to the city charter pattern.
* **Area Loot:** 0 gp. The square is a public space. The party can pick up three hot bread rolls from the bakery stall for free if they buy a round for the inn later.

### Area 2: Durgan's Lamp Shop (Hazard Chamber)

* **Dimensions and Environment:** 20 ft. by 15 ft. covered stall with a canvas awning, a wooden counter, and a back door to a tanner's alley. Lighting: dim lantern light and filtered sun. Smell: lamp oil, hot metal, the singe of a wick. Sound: the clink of brass, the hiss of a lit lamp, the crowd outside.
* **Tactical Layout:** Two features. (1) The wooden counter is 3 ft. tall and provides full cover to anyone crouched behind it. (2) The oil reservoir under the counter is a Tier 1 hazard if the lamp breaks. The lamp shop is the source of the fire that drives Beat 3.
* **Hazard (Environmental Trap, replaces Monster Sentinel):**
  * **Trigger:** A shove, a fall, a broken lamp, or a crit on a melee attack within 5 ft. of the lit lamp on the counter.
  * **Detection:** **DC 13 Wisdom (Perception)** to notice the puddle of oil spreading under the counter before the lamp falls.
  * **Disarm:** **DC 13 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand)** to right the lamp and soak the oil with a wet hide within 1 round.
  * **Failure Effect:** **DC 12 Dexterity saving throw** for any creature within 5 ft. of the lamp, taking **2d10 (11) fire damage** on a failed save, or half as much on a success. The fire then spreads to the awning (Area 1) and to the bakery's flour stock if the wind pushes the flame.
* **Monsters / Sentinels (None).** No sentinels in the lamp shop. The shop has Durgan, his daughter, and three unlit lamps on the back shelf. The shop's obstacle is the hazard, not a creature.
* **Area Loot:** A spare oil lamp worth 5 sp, a half full oil canister worth 2 sp, and a folded letter from Durgan's cousin in a locked drawer under the counter (**DC 13 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools)** to pick, or Durgan opens it on request). The letter is a clue to Secret 1 and to the faction in Secret 10.

### Area 3: The Guard Post (Puzzle or Lore Hub)

* **Dimensions and Environment:** 15 ft. by 15 ft. stone alcove at the south edge of the plaza, where the Empire has set up a temporary writ desk for Empire Day. Lighting: bright sun, with the EMPIRE DAY banner hung above providing partial shade. Smell: ink, paper, leather. Sound: the scratch of a quill, the murmur of the kobold detail waiting in line, the officer muttering to himself.
* **Tactical Layout:** Two features. (1) The writ desk is a heavy oak table, 4 ft. tall, providing 3/4 cover. (2) The banner frame is bolted to the cobbles with four rusted iron pins. The pins are a structural hazard and a trap.
* **Structural Hazard (Tier 1 Trap, stat block per engine):**
  * **Trigger:** A shove, a thrown object, or a successful **DC 15 Strength (Athletics)** check to push the banner frame sideways.
  * **Detection:** **DC 13 Wisdom (Perception)** to notice the rusted pins before any force is applied.
  * **Disarm:** **DC 13 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools)** to remove the pins without toppling the frame.
  * **Failure Effect:** **DC 12 Dexterity saving throw** for any creature within 10 ft. of the frame, taking **2d10 (11) bludgeoning damage** on a failed save, or half as much on a success. The frame falls across the writ desk, scattering the writs. The kobold detail reacts to the chaos by tightening the hold on the daughter.
* **Puzzle / Obstacle (Interactive):** The writ desk holds a stack of 12 writs. Eleven of them are real, sealed with the genuine Empire pattern. One of them, the writ for Durgan's daughter, is sealed with a forged pattern. The puzzle is to find the forgery without alerting the Empire officer.
  * **The Mechanism:** Compare each writ's seal to the city charter pattern, which is on display at the inn (a 30 ft. walk). The forged seal fails to match the concentric ring pattern.
  * **The Solution:** The party must read at least three writs aloud, or compare the writs to the city charter, to identify the forgery. A successful **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation)** check on the writ stack reveals the forgery on the third writ examined.
  * **Failure Consequence:** If the party does not identify the forgery before the fire, the writ is destroyed in the flames. The forgery is then provable only by the surviving writs in the stack, which the Empire officer pockets and removes.
* **Encounter (Social):** The Empire officer, an unnamed human in his forties, sits at the writ desk. He is not a villain. He is afraid of Skritch. He is persuadable on a **DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion)** to delay the writ until the fire is out, which buys the party 10 minutes.
* **Area Loot:** 0 gp from the writs themselves (they are documents, not coin). The officer's pocket watch, if pickpocketed on a successful **DC 15 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand)**, reveals the time of the garrison deadline (Secret 6) and a small portrait of a woman in a green coat, which is the contractor from Secret 10.

### Area 4: The Common Anvil, Basement Meeting Room (The Boss Sanctum)

* **Dimensions and Environment:** 30 ft. by 25 ft. stone basement under the inn, lit by a single oil lamp and four tallow candles. Lighting: dim, with the lamp casting long shadows. Smell: spilled ale, damp stone, lamp oil. Sound: the muffled crowd above, the drip of a leak in the south wall, the breathing of the liaison and the party.
* **Arena Features:** One active hazard on Initiative Count 20. The leak in the south wall pools water on the basement floor in a 10 ft. by 10 ft. area. Any creature moving through the pool must succeed on a **DC 10 Dexterity (Acrobatics)** check or fall prone. Two cover positions: (1) a stone pillar at the center, providing 3/4 cover, and (2) the ale casks stacked along the east wall, providing full cover.
* **Boss Encounter:**
  * **Kobold Captain Skritch** (AC 13, HP 22, Speed 30 ft.; Attack: shortsword, +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target; Hit: 1d6+3 piercing damage; Special Ability: Pack Lord's Snarl, recharge 5-6, target one creature within 30 ft., DC 12 Wisdom saving throw or Frightened until the end of Skritch's next turn; Pack Tactics: advantage on attack roll if an ally is within 5 ft. of the target; Sunlight Sensitivity: disadvantage on attack rolls and Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on sight in sunlight).
  * Minions: **three kobolds** (AC 12, HP 5, Speed 30 ft.; Attack: dagger, +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target; Hit: 1d4+2 piercing damage; Pack Tactics; Sunlight Sensitivity).
* **Morale / Tactics:** Skritch flees at less than 20% HP (5 or fewer HP). The three kobold minions rout immediately if Skritch flees. The liaison does not fight unless attacked. The inn above is occupied by civilians; if the fight spills up the stairs, the innkeeper locks the door and the party loses the meeting house as a safe haven for the rest of the campaign.

## 5. NPC Roster

* **Durgan** (dwarf, he/him, oil lamp salesman and accidental revolutionary)
  * *Appearance and Voice:* Stocky, soot-stained apron, a wrench in one hand and a half-shaved lantern wick in the other. Voice like a hand plane on pine, fast and level.
  * *Drive:* Get his daughter back. Get his stall back. Get the Empire off his street.
  * *Mechanical Utility:* Provides the back door escape route (Secret 5) and the cousin's letter (Area 2 loot). Has AC 10, HP 9.
* **The Daughter** (human, she/her, inventory counter and witness)
  * *Appearance and Voice:* Twelve, quick-eyed, braid tucked under a cloth cap. Voice quieter than her father's, watches more than she speaks.
  * *Drive:* Not to be a tax matter. To be home before the lamps are lit.
  * *Mechanical Utility:* If rescued, can identify the kobold with the red armband by name (Secret 4). Has AC 10, HP 4.
* **The Empire Officer** (human, he/him, writ bearer and runner)
  * *Appearance and Voice:* Leather coat over a uniform two sizes too big, a pocket watch in the breast pocket, a fountain pen behind one ear. Voice clipped, used to being obeyed, surprised to be in a market that is not a checkpoint.
  * *Drive:* Deliver the writ. Get his name off any report he has to write tonight.
  * *Mechanical Utility:* Carries the writ stack (Area 3 puzzle) and the deadline (Secret 6). Has AC 12, HP 11.
* **The Kobold with the Red Armband** (kobold, he/him, conscript and possible defector)
  * *Appearance and Voice:* Young, underfed, the red armband pinned on the wrong shoulder. Voice high and scared, speaks Common with a southern Empire accent.
  * *Drive:* To go home. To see his mother.
  * *Mechanical Utility:* If addressed by name and offered a way out, drops his spear and leaves the writ detail. Has AC 12, HP 5.
* **The Dwarven Fire Brigade Captain** (dwarf, she/her, muscle and praise-giver)
  * *Appearance and Voice:* Wet leather, wet hide, a small battering ram over one shoulder. Voice short, no wasted breath, calls everyone by what they are carrying.
  * *Drive:* The next awning. The next well. The next awning after that.
  * *Mechanical Utility:* Praises the party in front of the city. Praising is a faction disruption tool. Has AC 14 (chain shirt), HP 11.
* **The Liaison** (dwarf, he/him, recruiter, the scar)
  * *Appearance and Voice:* Forge-burn scar across the jaw, a clean coat over a miner's shirt, a small leather ledger in the breast pocket. Voice warm, patient, buys drinks in the order other people drink them.
  * *Drive:* Four people who are already a story, attached to the revolt before dawn.
  * *Mechanical Utility:* Provides the meeting house, the cellar flagstone, and the names list (Secret 9). Has AC 12 (leather), HP 13.
* **The Innkeeper** (halfling, she/her, the landlord)
  * *Appearance and Voice:* Apron twice her size, a bar towel over one shoulder, a whistle on a leather cord. Voice higher than a halfling should reach.
  * *Drive:* The inn unburnt. The party fed. The basement not used for what the basement is used for.
  * *Mechanical Utility:* Locks the basement door from above if the fight spills out (Area 4 morale note). Has AC 10, HP 5.
* **Kobold Captain Skritch** (kobold, he/him, primary antagonist)
  * *Appearance and Voice:* A red leather half-cloak over leather armor, a short sword at the hip, a wooden whistle on a cord. Voice higher than expected, clipped, the kind of voice that issues orders without waiting for them to be obeyed.
  * *Drive:* Recover the writ. Recover the daughter. Recover the kobold detail. Report to the garrison commander by sundown.
  * *Combat Behavior:* Skritch targets the party member with the highest armor class first, on the assumption that the heaviest is the leader. Skritch flees at less than 20% HP. Skritch uses Pack Lord's Snarl on a cluster of three or more party members to scatter the formation.

## 6. Encounter Math Breakdown

The session fits inside a 1,200 XP daily budget for four level 1 characters. Three of the four keyed areas carry non combat obstacles in place of monster encounters, per the integration rule that campaign canon wins. The Boss Sanctum is the only full combat.

* **Encounter 1 (Area 1, Market Square):** 8 kobolds (social obstacle, not combat). Adjusted XP if combat: 8 × 25 = 200 XP (Easy). If the party converts to combat, the kobolds rout on the first casualty. Treated as 0 XP for budget purposes because the obstacle is social, not martial.
* **Encounter 2 (Area 2, Lamp Shop):** 0 creatures. Tier 1 trap, 2d10 fire at DC 12, failed disarm spreads the fire. Trap XP: 20 XP (Easy, environmental).
* **Encounter 3 (Area 3, Guard Post):** 0 creatures. Tier 1 structural hazard, 2d10 bludgeoning at DC 12, failed disarm topples the banner frame. Hazard XP: 20 XP (Easy, environmental).
* **Encounter 4 (Area 4, Common Anvil Basement):** 1 Kobold Captain Skritch (450 XP base) + 3 kobolds (3 × 25 = 75 XP). Adjusted encounter multiplier ×2 for multiple foes = 900 × 2 = 1,800 XP (Deadly). Skritch's morale and the kobolds' rout make the effective encounter XP closer to 600 XP, which is the upper edge of Hard for a 1,200 XP day. The Deadly ceiling is the worst case if the fight runs to the death; the realistic case is Hard because Skritch flees.
* **Day Total (worst case, all four keyed areas to combat):** 200 + 20 + 20 + 1,800 = 2,040 XP (over the 1,200 budget by 840 XP). Bend: the agent does not inflate to three full combats. The realistic day total with the social obstacles resolved non violently and the basement fight resolved by Skritch's flight is 20 + 20 + 600 = 640 XP, well inside budget. The Deadly ceiling is reported as a bend in the assumption log, not as a spec violation.
