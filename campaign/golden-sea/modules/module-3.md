# FairWhether, Shopping and Information Gathering

**Level:** 2
**Setting:** FairWhether lower city, two days after the dawn assault, the day before the courier flight to Braaken. The market square, the forger's shop, the back-alley clinic, and the Tidal Lantern.
**Main Threat:** The Empire occupation in its quieter form, the patrol rotations the party collected in Session 1, the kobold pickpocket rings that have sprung up since the assault, and the rumor that Garruk has been seen in the city
**Adventure Summary:** The party has two days of breathing room before the courier flight, and the rebellion's quartermaster has a list. If the party does not spend the coin and the time well, the courier flight stages undergeared and the road to Braaken gets harder than it has to be.

---

## 1. Strong Start

> The market square is louder than it was on Empire Day, because Empire Day had Empire, and the Empire is bottled up in the garrison now, and a market without occupation is a market with an opinion. The stalls are open, the prices are half what they were a week ago, and a dwarf selling ironwork is shouting at a halfling selling ribbon about whose stall is closer to the well, and the argument is the kind of argument that ends with both of them buying each other a drink.

* **Active Obstacle:** None required. The session opens in peacetime. If the table wants a fight, an optional Trivial encounter awaits at the Tidal Lantern with a kobold pickpocket ring that the party can ignore, deter, or take down.
* **Tactical Objective:** Spend the session's coin and time. Buy gear. Sell loot. Recruit. Rest. The party's goal is to leave FairWhether on the courier flight with the best possible loadout, the best possible intelligence about the road, and the best possible reputation with the lower city dwarves.
* **Transition Trigger:** The liaison arrives at the Tidal Lantern in the evening of the second day with the courier flight manifest and a sealed letter from Holrik. The letter names the road to Braaken and the Empire's response to the dawn assault. The party has the rest of the evening to finalize preparations.

## 2. Universal Hooks

The four hooks are rewritten from the engine template to fit the FairWhether campaign. None are rolled. All four are active in this session as facts the party can discover.

1. **The Bounty (rewritten as The Quarry Map).** A dwarf stonecutter named Bodrik has a quarry map of the road to Paradiso. The map shows three bandit camps and one Empire waystation. The liaison will pay 20 gp for the map. Bounty equivalent: 20 gp for the map.
2. **The Distress Call (rewritten as The Sick Apprentice).** Wren, the halfling dock worker's apprentice rescued from the pier office in Session 2, has taken a fever in the back alley clinic. The clinic is a back room in a halfling herbalist's shop, and the herbalist needs coin for medicine. Distress call: Wren is alive, sick, and the herbalist will work for 10 gp.
3. **The Unsealed Ruin (rewritten as The Forger's Back Room).** A dwarf forger in the lower city has a back room with a forge and a small library of Empire seal patterns. The forger is the person who made the Multipass. The unsealing is the back room, and the forger can produce one additional document per long rest for the party, for a fee.
4. **The Direct Ambush (rewritten as The Pickpocket Ring).** A kobold pickpocket ring has formed in the lower city since the dawn assault. The ring is led by a deserter from the Empire garrison, a kobold named Skritch's former second in command. The ambush is the pickpocket ring, and the party can deter them with a single Intimidation check or take them down in Area 4.

## 3. Ten Secrets and Clues

Ten concrete facts about FairWhether, the courier flight, the road to Braaken, and the Empire's response to the dawn assault. Each is discoverable in play through the listed check or social action.

1. **City Origin (rewritten from Dungeon Origin).** FairWhether's lower city was rebuilt after a fire in the fourth age of the dwarven calendar. The fire was set by an Empire predecessor and is the reason the lower city has a granite foundation under the wooden upper floors. Discovery: **DC 10 Wisdom (History)** or asking any of the older dwarves in the market.
2. **Structural Hazard (rewritten from Structural Hazard).** The back alley clinic is in a building with a sagging second floor. The second floor is held up by a single rotten beam. The beam will collapse if more than 200 pounds is placed on the second floor at once. Discovery: **DC 10 Wisdom (Perception)** when entering the clinic.
3. **Boss Vulnerability (rewritten from Boss Vulnerability).** The kobold pickpocket ring's leader, a kobold named Vren, is afraid of Skritch. Vren deserted the garrison when the relief column was repelled. If the party mentions Skritch by name, Vren will offer to disband the ring in exchange for not being reported. Discovery: **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight)** the first time the party encounters the pickpocket ring, or by asking Pip.
4. **Faction Disruption (rewritten from Faction Disruption).** The forger who made the Multipass is a dwarf named Kessa. Kessa is a quiet supporter of the rebellion, but Kessa is also a quiet supporter of the Empire's seal library. Kessa can be turned to exclusive rebellion support on a successful **DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion)** check, or her loyalty can be split with a smaller payment. Discovery: **DC 13 Charisma (Insight)** at the forger's shop, or by asking the liaison.
5. **Secret Passage (rewritten from Secret Passage).** The back alley clinic has a trapdoor under the herbalist's drying rack. The trapdoor opens to a drainage channel that runs under the lower city and emerges at the inner pier. The channel is the courier flight's emergency exit if the Empire retakes the lower city. Discovery: **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation)** at the back of the clinic, or the herbalist shows the party.
6. **Impending Doom (rewritten from Impending Doom).** The Empire garrison sends a second relief column from the imperial capital at the end of the week, no later than 7 in game days from the start of the session. The second column is 50 kobolds plus 10 humans in heavy armor, plus a war drum and a single mage. The courier flight must be out of FairWhether by Day 7 or the party is caught in the second column's assault. Discovery: **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation)** on the Empire patrol rotations the party collected in Session 1, or by asking Halga.
7. **Puzzle Solution (rewritten from Puzzle Solution).** The forger's back room library contains the Empire seal pattern library, but the library is locked behind a dwarven puzzle box. The puzzle box requires a three word phrase to open. The phrase is the name of the Empire's founder, written in Dwarvish. The herbalist knows the phrase because the herbalist used to work in the Empire trading post. Discovery: **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation)** on the puzzle box, or by asking the herbalist.
8. **Guardian Lore (rewritten from Guardian Lore).** The back alley clinic is run by a halfling herbalist named Dalla. Dalla has been running the clinic since the Empire occupation began, and Dalla has treated Empire soldiers and rebellion fighters with the same herbs. Dalla's neutrality is the clinic's protection. Discovery: asking Dalla, or **DC 10 Intelligence (History)** in the lower city.
9. **Hidden Vault (rewritten from Hidden Vault).** The forger's back room has a loose brick in the chimney. The brick covers a small iron box containing 60 gp, the forger's emergency fund, and a list of Empire seal patterns not yet in the library. Discovery: **DC 13 Wisdom (Perception)** in the back room, or Kessa shows the party.
10. **The Next Threat (rewritten from The Next Threat).** Garruk, the half orc Empire spy from Session 1, has been seen in the city. Garruk is not Empire. Garruk is EoE. EoE is a company of specialists, ex rebellion, ex anything, who do the jobs armies cannot admit to. Garruk is operating under a new cover, and the new cover is a tinker who has set up a stall in the market square. Discovery: **DC 15 Charisma (Investigation)** in the market, or by asking Pip.

## 4. Keyed Spatial Locations

The four keyed areas are mapped to the session's real locations. The first three carry social and investigative scenes in place of monster encounters, per the campaign canon's low key intent. The fourth is the Tidal Lantern, the session's evening scene with the liaison and the courier flight manifest, and an optional Trivial encounter with the kobold pickpocket ring.

### Area 1: The Market Square (Entry Point)

* **Dimensions and Environment:** 80 ft. by 60 ft. open cobble plaza, ringed by awnings on three sides, with the well at the north end and the EMPIRE DAY banner frame still standing at the center. Lighting: bright afternoon sun. Smell: lamp oil, hot bread, fresh ironwork. Sound: a fiddler, a crowd murmur, the bark of Bodrik the stonecutter, the halfling ribbon seller arguing with him.
* **Tactical Layout:** Two features. (1) The well at the north end provides 3/4 cover. (2) The banner frame at the center is still standing, and the bolts are still rusted. The frame is a structural hazard if shoved.
* **Encounter (Social):** Bodrik the stonecutter, a dwarf in a leather apron with a quarry map of the road to Paradiso. Bodrik will sell the map for 20 gp, or trade it for a service. The party can also recruit Bodrik as a guide for the courier flight, which is what the liaison wants. Discovery: **DC 10 Wisdom (Insight)** to notice the map in Bodrik's belt pouch, or by asking the liaison.
* **Investigative DCs:**
  * **DC 10 Wisdom (Perception):** the bolts on the banner frame are rusted through.
  * **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation):** a tinker at a stall across the square is watching the party without selling anything. The tinker is Garruk.
* **Area Loot:** 0 gp from the market itself. The party can purchase gear at standard prices (DMG table) and sell loot at 50% value.

### Area 2: The Forger's Shop (Hazard Chamber)

* **Dimensions and Environment:** 20 ft. by 15 ft. stone shop with a forge in the back room and a small library of seal patterns on a wooden shelf. Lighting: dim, with the forge casting long shadows. Smell: hot metal, ink, the waxy smell of sealing wax. Sound: the clink of a hammer, the hiss of quenched metal, the low voice of Kessa the forger.
* **Tactical Layout:** Two features. (1) The forge is a Tier 1 hazard if knocked over. The forge is a small stone forge with a low fire. (2) The seal pattern library is locked behind a dwarven puzzle box on the shelf.
* **Trap / Environmental Hazard (The Forge):**
  * **Trigger:** A shove, a critical hit on a melee attack within 5 ft. of the forge, or a successful **DC 15 Strength (Athletics)** check to overturn the forge.
  * **Detection:** **DC 13 Wisdom (Perception)** to notice the forge is unstable.
  * **Disarm:** **DC 13 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand)** to bank the fire and stabilize the forge within 1 round.
  * **Failure Effect:** **DC 12 Dexterity saving throw** for any creature within 5 ft. of the forge, taking **2d10 (11) fire damage** on a failed save, or half as much on a success. The forge is then out and the forger's back room is unusable for 1 day.
* **Monsters / Sentinels:** None. Kessa the forger, a dwarf woman in a leather apron with a small hammer, is the only other person in the shop.
* **Area Loot:** 60 gp from the loose brick in the chimney (Secret 9), plus one additional Multipass document produced by Kessa for the cost of 10 gp and 1 hour of work. The party can produce up to 2 additional documents over the two day session by paying Kessa and making the check.

### Area 3: The Back Alley Clinic (Puzzle or Lore Hub)

* **Dimensions and Environment:** 20 ft. by 15 ft. back room in a halfling herbalist's shop, lit by a single oil lamp and a small hearth. Lighting: dim, with a drying rack hanging from the ceiling. Smell: dried herbs, lamp oil, fever sweat, the metallic smell of a sickroom. Sound: the creak of the drying rack, the small cough of Wren in the corner, the low voice of Dalla the herbalist.
* **Tactical Layout:** Two features. (1) The drying rack is hung from the ceiling by ropes tied to the rotten second floor beam. The rack is a structural hazard if overloaded. (2) The trapdoor under the drying rack is concealed by a rug.
* **Interactive Puzzle / Obstacle (The Forger's Library Puzzle Box):**
  * **The Mechanism:** The forger's seal pattern library is locked behind a dwarven puzzle box, a small wooden box with three rotating letter dials. The combination is a three word phrase: the name of the Empire's founder, written in Dwarvish. The phrase is "Balin son of Fundin." Dalla the herbalist knows the phrase.
  * **The Solution:** Three workable paths. (a) The party can ask Dalla for the phrase, requiring a **DC 10 Charisma (Persuasion)** check (Dalla gives it freely on a successful check, and on a failed check Dalla demands to know what the party is doing with Empire seals). (b) The party can pick the lock with thieves' tools, requiring a **DC 15 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools)** check. (c) The party can break the box with force, requiring a **DC 15 Strength (Athletics)** check, which destroys the library.
  * **Failure Consequence:** If the party picks or breaks the box unsuccessfully, the library is not accessible. If the party breaks the box and the library is the rebellion's only copy of certain Empire seal patterns, the rebellion loses those patterns for the rest of the campaign.
* **Encounter (Social):** Dalla the herbalist, a halfling woman in a stained apron, treats Wren in the corner. Dalla will accept 10 gp for the medicine Wren needs. Dalla will also accept a service, a favor, or a future debt. Dalla is neutral and will not take sides.
* **Area Loot:** 0 gp from the clinic itself. The trapdoor under the drying rack is the courier flight's emergency exit and is worth 0 gp but is a Session 4 thread.

### Area 4: The Tidal Lantern, Evening (The Boss Sanctum)

* **Dimensions and Environment:** 40 ft. by 30 ft. dock tavern, dimly lit by lanterns and the kitchen fire. Lighting: dim, with the evening sun on the terrace. Smell: ale, fried fish, salt, the coal smoke of the kitchen. Sound: the low hum of rebellion soldiers and dockworkers on their second drink, the clink of cups, the lap of water under the terrace, the quiet argument between two pickpockets in the back.
* **Arena Features:** One active hazard on Initiative Count 20. The terrace railing is rotten on the south side (see module-2's Area 4). Two cover positions: the long bar and the back room doorway.
* **Optional Trivial Encounter (The Kobold Pickpocket Ring):**
  * **Vren** (AC 12, HP 5, Speed 30 ft.; Attack: dagger, +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target; Hit: 1d4+2 piercing damage; Pack Tactics: not applicable alone; Sunlight Sensitivity: disadvantage on attack rolls and Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on sight in sunlight).
  * Minions: **two kobolds** (AC 12, HP 5, Speed 30 ft.; Attack: dagger, +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target; Hit: 1d4+2 piercing damage; Pack Tactics; Sunlight Sensitivity).
* **Morale / Tactics:** Vren and the two kobolds pick the party's pockets on a successful **DC 13 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand)** check from the kobolds, and the party can deter them with a single **DC 13 Charisma (Intimidation)** check or take them down in a single round of combat. Vren surrenders if reduced to 0 HP and offers to disband the ring in exchange for not being reported. Skritch's name is the key: Vren fears Skritch and will not fight if Skritch is mentioned by name. The encounter is Trivial (75 XP base, x1 for few foes = 75 XP) and is optional.
* **Boss Sanctum Substitution:** This is the session's "Boss Sanctum" in name only. There is no required boss fight. The session's tension is the liaison's arrival, the courier flight manifest, and the party's final preparations. The liaison does not fight unless attacked. If Vren is present, the encounter is Trivial. The party can always end the session here.

## 6. Encounter Math Breakdown

The session fits inside a 2,400 XP daily budget for four level 2 characters. The session is low key. Most of the day is social and investigative. The day has one optional Trivial encounter at the Tidal Lantern.

* **Encounter 1 (Area 1, Market Square):** 0 creatures. Social scene. No XP.
* **Encounter 2 (Area 2, Forger's Shop):** 0 creatures. Social scene with a Tier 1 trap (the forge). Trap XP: 20 XP (Trivial, environmental).
* **Encounter 3 (Area 3, Back Alley Clinic):** 0 creatures. Social and puzzle scene. No XP.
* **Encounter 4 (Area 4, Tidal Lantern, optional):** 1 Vren + 2 kobolds. Adjusted XP with encounter multiplier x1 for few foes = 75 XP (Trivial). Vren surrenders at 0 HP, dropping the encounter to 0 XP.
* **Day Total (all areas social, no combat):** 20 XP (the forge trap, if triggered). Trivial.
* **Day Total (all areas social plus optional Trivial pickpocket ring):** 20 + 75 = 95 XP. Trivial, well inside the 2,400 XP budget.
* **Day Total (worst case, all four areas to combat, plus Garruk intervention):** 20 + 75 + 250 (Garruk, see below) = 345 XP. Easy, inside budget. Garruk is a CR 1/2 thug (100 XP) plus one kobold minion (25 XP), adjusted x2 = 250 XP. Garruk does not fight in this session per the campaign canon, so the worst case is hypothetical.
* **Bend:** The session's day total is 95 XP realistic, 345 XP worst case. The 2,400 XP budget is honored by a factor of 7 to 25. The engine's three to four combat encounter assumption is unsatisfiable without violating the campaign canon's low key intent. The session is a deliberate underdog of combat, which is the master doc's intent.
