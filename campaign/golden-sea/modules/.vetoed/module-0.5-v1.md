# Session 0.5 — FairWhether Market

## The Hook

A mountain city throws a market the way a fist throws a punch. Every stall is a shout, every alley is a throat, and the dwarves of FairWhether know how to make a thousand small sounds feel like one enormous welcome. The party walks in on Empire Day, the one afternoon a year when the cobbles belong to the people and not to the boots, and the oil lamps come out because oil lamps are what the dwarves sell, and oil lamps are what they light when they want a festival to feel like the inside of a forge.

There is a girl at a stall. Her father is selling lamps next to her. The Empire has rules for Empire Day. The Empire also has kobolds in uniform, and the kobolds have a writ, and the writ says the girl comes with them for a tax matter that nobody can read because nobody wrote it. The girl's father is Durgan, and Durgan is the kind of man who argues with his hands, and a lamp gets knocked, and a lamp gets broken, and a lamp gets lit in a puddle of lamp oil on a stone floor, and the fire is a small god for the four minutes it lives.

The party is now a story the dwarves will tell. The party is also a problem the Empire will solve. Both of these things happen in the same hour. Neither of them un-happens.

## Timeline

Total: 3h55. The session is one continuous market afternoon and the long evening after. There is no travel, no time skip, no off-screen rumor dump. The party's first kill is in this hour. So is their first fire. So is their first taste of what the Empire looks like up close.

## Scenes

### Beat 1 — Market floor (0:00 to 0:45)

Location: the broad stone plaza in the center of FairWhether, ringed by awnings and stalls. The four players arrive separately. Give each one a reason to be there that is not rebellion. A sword that needs a whetstone. A letter to deliver. A cousin who runs a bread stall. A debt to collect. The festival is the cover for every small errand in the city. Use that.

Who is present: roughly three hundred dwarves, a dozen humans, four halflings selling ribbons, the Empire detail of nine kobolds and one human officer under a hanging banner that says EMPIRE DAY in three languages. Durgan is at his stall, oil lamps hung in rows like fruit. His daughter, who has no name in the source, is helping him count inventory.

What the players can interact with: any stall, the Empire banner, the kobold patrol, Durgan's lamps, the well at the north end of the plaza, the bakery on the east side, the alley mouth behind Durgan's stall.

What goes wrong if they do nothing: they have a pleasant afternoon. The session ends without a hook. If you sense the table drifting toward shopping, escalate Beat 2 by twenty minutes.

### Beat 2 — The writ (0:45 to 1:25)

Location: Durgan's stall.

Who is present: Durgan, his daughter, four kobolds, the human officer with the writ, the four players, and a thickening ring of dwarves who close in the way water closes in around a dropped stone.

What happens: the kobolds walk to the stall and read a writ. The writ is in Imperial Common, which the kobolds can speak badly and read worse. The writ claims Durgan owes a back tax on lamp oil from the winter. The actual reason is that Durgan's cousin is a rebel courier and the Empire wants leverage. The party does not know the real reason. The party will not learn it for two acts. Let the writ be what it appears to be on the page: bureaucratic, small, mean.

The kobolds take the daughter by the arm. Durgan argues. Durgan reaches for a lamp to set it between his daughter and the kobolds. A kobold shoves Durgan. The lamp falls. The lamp breaks. The oil spreads.

Player interaction: anyone can intervene. Anyone can choose to read the writ themselves. Anyone can try to talk the kobolds down. The kobolds have orders. Talking down does not work. The lamp is the pivot, not the argument.

What goes wrong if they do nothing: the kobolds take the daughter, the Empire officer leaves with her, and the party is left holding a market stall in a city that just taught them what it is. Not catastrophic, but the session loses its fire and its first kill. If the table hesitates, an NPC in the crowd shoves a kobold by accident. The shove is the spark. You do not need to invent the players' courage for them. You need to give them a moment where hesitation costs more than action.

### Beat 3 — The fire (1:25 to 2:05)

Location: Durgan's stall, then the surrounding four stalls, then the awning over the bread seller.

Who is present: the four players, Durgan, the daughter, the kobold patrol, the human officer, three hundred marketgoers, and the fire.

What happens: lamp oil on stone is the slowest of the fast fires. It crawls. It finds the awning. The awning finds the bread seller's dried flour. The fire is loud now and it is loud in the way a crowd is loud, a sound with weight. The kobolds do not fight the fire. The kobolds leave the daughter, leave the writ on the ground, leave their officer standing with his mouth open, and run. The officer runs last. The daughter runs toward Durgan. The crowd runs outward.

Player interaction: the players choose what to save. The bakery has a child in it. The well is forty feet away. The writ is on the ground and is now a single sheet of burning paper. The next stall over is a tinker with a forge and a forge fire. If the players do not act on the forge, the forge goes next and the fire triples in a minute.

What goes wrong if they do nothing: the fire takes the row, the brigade arrives to a block already lost, and the party is on the curb watching instead of inside the story. Push them in with a civilian grabbing a player by the sleeve and begging.

### Beat 4 — The brigade (2:05 to 2:50)

Location: the market, the alley mouth, the well.

Who is present: the dwarven fire brigade, twelve dwarves in wet leather carrying wet hides and a small battering ram for shuttered windows. The party. The crowd. The kobolds, far away by now, not coming back.

What happens: the brigade works. The brigade works the way dwarves work, which is to say without asking anyone's permission and without explaining. The party gets recruited by being handed a bucket. If a player refuses the bucket, the brigade gives them two buckets. The brigade does not care about heroics. The brigade cares about wet hides and the next awning. Praise comes later and it comes from Durgan, not from the brigade.

Player interaction: bucket, hide, ram, well, alley, civilian. The party is now part of the city's muscle memory. The dwarves will remember this. The Empire will remember this too, and the Empire's memory is the part that runs on paper.

What goes wrong if they do nothing: nothing, in this beat. The brigade handles the fire. If the party stands down here, the session still moves forward, but the praise at Beat 6 lands softer. The party needs the praise for the recruit to bite.

### Beat 5 — The boy in uniform (2:50 to 3:25)

Location: a side alley, two streets south of the plaza, behind a tannery.

Who is present: the four players, one kobold, and the Empire officer, who is trying to reach a side gate with the writ stuffed inside his coat.

What happens: the Empire officer is not a villain. The Empire officer is a man with a wife and a writ and a kobold conscript he does not like, ordered to retrieve a girl from a market for a reason he was not told. He is leaving. The kobold rear guard is a boy. The boy has a short sword and shaking hands. The boy is told to hold the alley. The boy is told that if the party comes, he is to make them stop.

The party comes. The party is louder than a boy. The boy is the party's first kill if the party crosses the alley. The boy is also a death the table will feel, and the table should feel it, and that feeling is the point. The Empire is not cartoon evil. The Empire is administrative evil. The boy is what administrative evil looks like when it stands between you and a door.

Player interaction: the players can talk the boy down. The players can go around. The players can cross the alley. The players can take the boy alive. The boy does not want to die. The boy wants to go home. The boy is fifteen and has been in the Empire garrison for three months and does not know why.

What goes wrong if they do nothing: the officer escapes with the writ, the Empire has a paper trail on the market incident, and the recruit at Beat 6 has a tighter window because the Empire will move first. The kobold boy lives, and the party is not yet a story the city tells. This is survivable. The session does not require the kill. The session does require a cost. Make sure whatever choice the table makes has a cost the table can name.

### Beat 6 — The recruit (3:25 to 3:55)

Location: the inn at the south end of the market street, the Common Anvil. The sign is a real anvil. The door is the kind of door that has been repaired more than it has been replaced.

Who is present: the four players, Durgan, the daughter, the dwarven fire brigade captain, and the liaison. The liaison is the dwarf with the forge-burn scar across the jaw. He does not give his name in the first five minutes. He buys the first round.

What happens: the party is fed. The party is given a room. The party is given the story the dwarves are already telling, which is that four strangers fought the fire and held the alley and either killed or spared a kobold boy, and whichever they did, the city is going to remember it. The liaison waits until the second round to ask the question. The question is short. The question is: are you in.

Player interaction: the players can refuse. The players can ask what in means. The players can ask for payment. The players can ask to leave the city in the morning. The liaison answers all of this with the same answer, which is that the city gates close at dawn and the Empire is putting names on a list and the names include theirs.

What goes wrong if they do nothing: the party eats, sleeps, and wakes to Empire soldiers at the inn door. The session ends in custody. The next session is a jailbreak instead of a revolt. This is a valid run. It is not the run the campaign wants. If the table refuses the recruit, the campaign pivots to a darker Act I and the party enters the rebellion as escapees rather than volunteers. Both are playable. Neither is wrong. The owner should know which one the table is choosing and adjust the next module's opening.

## NPCs

**Durgan**
Role: oil lamp salesman, father, accidental revolutionary.
Voice: loud, hands-first, says lamp when he means life.
What he wants: his daughter back, his stall back, the Empire off his street.
What he hides: his cousin is a rebel courier. He has not told the party. He may not tell the party for two acts.
Alive at session end: yes.

**The daughter**
Role: inventory counter, hostage, witness.
Voice: quieter than her father, watches more than she speaks.
What she wants: to not be a tax matter.
What she hides: she has been counting Empire patrol rotations for her father's cousin for a year. She is twelve.
Alive at session end: yes.

**The Empire officer**
Role: writ bearer, kidnapper, runner.
Voice: clipped, used to being obeyed, surprised to be in a market that is not a checkpoint.
What he wants: the writ delivered, his name off any report he has to write tonight.
What he hides: he does not know why the writ was written. He was told it was a back tax. He believes it.
Alive at session end: yes if the party lets him run, no if they do not. Both are valid.

**The kobold boy**
Role: rear guard, conscript, first kill.
Voice: high, scared, speaks Common with a southern Empire accent.
What he wants: to go home.
What he hides: he is the youngest of six and his mother sends him letters that the garrison chaplain does not always deliver.
Alive at session end: at the table's discretion.

**The kobold patrol (eight others)**
Role: escort, then runners, then a memory.
Voice: the patrol does not talk to the party. The patrol talks to itself.
What they want: to not be on fire.
What they hide: most of them are conscripts. The one with the red armband is not.
Alive at session end: yes, all of them.

**The dwarven fire brigade captain**
Role: muscle, praise-giver, first dwarf the party meets who is not a merchant.
Voice: short sentences, no wasted breath, calls everyone by what they are carrying.
What she wants: the next awning, the next well, the next awning after that.
What she hides: nothing. The captain is the cleanest NPC in the module. Use her as a tonal anchor.
Alive at session end: yes.

**The liaison**
Role: recruiter, gatekeeper, the scar.
Voice: warm, patient, buys drinks in the order other people drink them.
What he wants: four people who are already a story, attached to the revolt before dawn.
What he hides: the revolt is not yet a revolt. The revolt is twelve dwarves and a cousin and a list of names. The liaison is selling a movement that does not yet exist.
Alive at session end: yes.

**The market crowd**
Role: three hundred witnesses, future narrators, the reason the city remembers.
Voice: many voices, one pressure.
What they want: the festival back, the fire out, the Empire off their street.
What they hide: nothing as a crowd. As individuals, some of them have already lost a child to a conscription lottery.
Alive at session end: all of them, unless the table picks a fight with the crowd, in which case the crowd handles it.

## Exits

The session has four valid endings and one failure state.

**Ending A — The clean recruit.** The party takes the liaison's offer, eats, sleeps, and wakes inside the rebellion. The next session opens on Day 2 with the party at the FairWhether storehouse for guard duty, hunting the spy the master doc names at Session 1. This is the run the campaign wants. Threads activated: ITEM X handoff, Multipass acquisition, the dawn dock assault, the road to Braaken.

**Ending B — The conditional recruit.** The party takes the offer but with conditions. Common conditions: payment, a guarantee the daughter is never used as a bargaining chip again, a meeting with the cousin. The liaison agrees to two of three. The third is a fight. Whatever the party settles, they enter the rebellion with a stated grievance. The next session opens on Day 2 with a small scene where the party's grievance is either honored or quietly sidestepped. Threads activated: same as A, with a watch-the-handler thread the liaison did not expect.

**Ending C — The arrest pivot.** The Empire officer escapes with the writ, the party refuses the recruit, and the party wakes to Empire soldiers. The next session is a jailbreak or a coerced conscription. The party enters the rebellion as escapees. Threads activated: Empire has paperwork on the party, ITEM X is harder to acquire, the liaison is harder to find, Glory's role shifts later.

**Ending D — The split.** The party refuses the recruit and the officer does not escape with the writ, so the Empire has no immediate paper trail. The party walks out the gates at dawn. The next session is a pursuit through the mountains with the Empire closing in and the liaison sending runners. The party enters the rebellion on foot, hunted, three days behind the schedule the campaign expects. Threads activated: the cousin meets them on the road, Glory meets them earlier, the FairWhether revolt proceeds without them and the city falls. This is a valid run. It is a sad run.

**Failure state — The party burns the plaza.** If the fire takes the row and the party does not help the brigade, the session ends with three dead dwarves and a city that will not forgive the party for a long time. The liaison still finds them. The liaison's offer is colder. The next session is the same jailbreak as C. Do not steer toward this ending. The module does not require heroism. It does require participation. If the table is going to stand on the curb, the table is going to stand on the curb with three names on a list.
