Rivals and Destiny

The page he built for it in 2020 says it plainly. Known in Zhork as The Radicals, four companions joined the global uprising against the Froscius Dynasty. They set out to help a young dwarven girl and it escalated into a treasonous world tour. Twenty five sessions followed. This is the record of getting them out of the room they were played in.

The World

Draega turns alone around a yellow sun, blue and green, cut into twelve continents by oceans that do most of the deciding. The year runs 360 days and the day runs twenty five hours. Somebody worked that out first and then built weather on top of it.

Two moons hang over it and neither is ever anything but full. They circle each other while they circle the world. Phoegrin is the red one. It brings the winter and closes every cycle, and it is known as the bringer of death. Aqgui is blue and thought to be made entirely of water, and it opens what the other one ends. Both give off their own light instead of borrowing the sun's, so night here comes down red and blue at once, and an eclipse floods the whole planet with whichever moon is in front.

The oceans are where the danger lives. A maelstrom of slow smoke and cloud turns in the middle of the Kladesh, and it is the first thing a sailor gets told. The White Ocean takes its name from the caps on its waves rather than from the frozen continent it wraps. Tides running up from the south collide in it, hard and without a pattern, which is why almost none of it is fit to sail and why it holds the deepest water on the world.

Ice takes both ends. The Greylands in the south belong to the Ice Giants, who govern nothing because nobody contests them, and rare elements sit under that ice with no way anyone has found to lift them. The Whitelands in the north are plain white and hold more life than the south, all of it hunting the ocean or each other.

In between, each continent does one thing well. Zhork is rock and volcano and dwarves, and it made the printing press. Imnia is fertile in the north and overrun by the undead in the south, and the only reason those two halves stay apart is a volcano range running east to west. Vernax is barren and full of metal, which is why the whole world wants it and why its own settlements keep going to war with each other.

The multiverse files all of this as a backwater. Every god has followers here and none in the numbers they draw elsewhere, and outsiders arrive by accident or by divine intervention and seldom any other way. Draega is far enough out that being left alone is its main feature. Which is worth knowing before four people start a war on it.
